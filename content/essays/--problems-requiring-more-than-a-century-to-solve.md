There's many problems in the world that arise from just living one lifespan.
What's the fundamental reason that we as a society cannot learn some things
effectively? 2000 year old texts still hold wisdom that it's impossible to
impart in an effective way, and we've not figured out a systematic method for
sharing this wisdom between generations.

For example: Overspecialise, and you breed in weakness. If everyone's too
specialised and there's not enough variance, then you're susceptible to a
targeted attack that takes out everyone. This is incredibly applicable to all
manner of things, from designing ML algorithms to team structure to personal
development to career planning to stock trading. But people make this mistake
_all the time_. I'm not interested in solutions that tell people to study this
material. I want to know why it is that this wisdom is seemingly unable to be
institutionalised, but other wisdom (like reading, writing, basic arithmetic)
_can_ be institutionalised. It's clear that we are able to build long-term
structures that educate vast swathes of the population, but why are some things
in here but other things aren't?

Also, I want to talk about the issues of motivation across multiple lifespans.
You could motivate high level politicians by requiring them to have children,
and then granting significant bonuses onto their children if the country is
doing well in 10, 20, 30 years time.
