Also exists for AI agents, where you can potentially gain capabilities by
having them work against each other

Also has references to GANs specifically and adversarial work in general.

This is a general phenomenon in situations where truth-seeking is valued,

It's useful to have two parties competing against one another, rather than hoping a single party can temper itself.

It's useful to not try and temper your opinions because your model of your
critics says that they'll think that you think XYZ. Just say what you feel, and
trust that they'll correct you if you're wrong. Your model of them is
inevitably lossy, so don't attempt to model them. Focus on yourself, and take
the critique when (if) it comes.

This can be external as well, with multiple government bodies or teams in a
company that have opposed goals. It's easier (but more wasteful) to have two
bodies that oppose each other, and you can trust that each of the bodies will
be pushing as hard as they can against the other. If you can assume there's no
foul play (which isn't always true, but sometimes it's true to a first
approximation) then the two parties will bring out all the arguments and
counter arguments, and you'll end up with a remarkably well-considered opinion.
Ideally this is how the judiciary system works, with a prosecutor and lawyer
working against each other. We don't have one person serving both roles,
because we know that competition will bring cause the good arguments to be
brought forward. It's not often appreciated that capitalism is a diluted
variant of this idea, that competition is wasteful (you have multiple parties
doing the same thing) but you do end up with a race to the top (i just hope you
defined "top" to be something reasonable, and not, you know, arbitrary status
tokens).
