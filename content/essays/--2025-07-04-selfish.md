- This works into Commoditise Your Complement: In that you can be competitive,
  but your best action is to do something altruistic for the whole society that
  gives you a larger moat and makes more people need your product.

- Sometimes entities (like bees or corporations) can have selfish motives (like
  making honey or profit) but those selfish motives are best served by pursuing
  cooperative behaviour such as pollinating flowers or building submarine
  cables.

- This is the core idea. There are some environments where agents end up
  pursuing goals that help other agents, possibly even agents with whom they're
  competing, because that's the best way to help themselves. Call this idea
  _???_.

- _???_ has been discussed before, but generally with a focus on systems which
  already have this mechanism in place. I'm interested in how to either build a
  system that has these incentives in-place, or otherwise mutate an existing
  system such that it ends up with these incentives.

- Let's try figure out what are the critical requirements for _???_. From
  there, we can test our hypothesis, making predictions and seeing if they
  hold. Once we've got a working hypothesis, let's theorise what we could
  change about existing hyper-competitive systems to move them towards the
  _???_ style systems.

- If you have two people sharing a pie, they can either battle over who gets
  more pie (maybe one of them is hungrier) or they can increase the size of the
  pie, or they can reduce the other's demand for the pie. There's a complement
  to the pie, if grandma is forcing you and your sibling to eat some bitter
  brussel sprouts, you can either battle over who has to eat more, or you can
  decrease the number of sprouts you have to eat, or you can increase the other
  person's demand for the sprouts.

  - Sometimes the phrasing _increasing the pie_ is misleading, as the _???_
    strategy can lead to a collective decrease in the risks rather than an
    increase of the benefits: A sheep joining a herd decreases the risk of
    perishing for both the existing members of the herd and the newcomer.

- If there is no way to change the pie, then we have a zero-sum game. But often
  real-world zero-sum games have permissible adaptions that create benefit
  without any cost. There's no guarantee that the benefit will last, or that
  it'll be shared by all players of the game, but it _is_ possible to take a
  real-world scenario that appears zero-sum and to convert it into something
  where, on average, everyone benefits. "permissible adaptions" meaning here
  that nobody's going to complain (or at least, they won't complain _much_) if
  you make a change to the game in a given way. Like the cyclists forming a
  peloton: there's nobody claiming that it's _unfair_ to form a peloton, even
  though it changes the game so much that forming a peloton is a critical
  strategy in modern cycling.

- I guess the way to look at it is that there's some resource I want, be it
  profit or honey or whatever. I can get more of it by either getting a larger
  fraction of what already exists, or I can keep my fraction the same but
  increase the total amount of it that exists. If I increase the total amount,
  I inadvertently help the others who might also want that resource, but I'm
  still incentivised to increase the total amount because my share is increased
  by this action.

  tries to ask what if we can take a zero-sum game and rephrase it as a
  positive sum game
