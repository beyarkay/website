> Sophistry
> [noun, plural: sophistries]
>
> The use of clever but false arguments with the goal of deception.

Sometimes, it's useful to know what techniques bad actors will use against you,
even if you intend on being fully rational.

This is index of sophistry techniques, to become comprehensive over time.
Nothing in here is about truth-seeking or rationally updating your beliefs
(for heaven's sake, just [read the sequences][1] already). This is all about
manipulating human biases in order to make your argument as convincing as
possible.

I have also found sophistry to be an incredibly useful term to describe
anti-rationality, for experts in this dark art truly aren't just un-rational,
but have a certain cunning which deserves it's own term so that it might be
called out and shot down on sight.

# Appeal to experts

# Give an [exploding offer][2]

This is an offer which your counter party has to accept immediately.

# Threaten legal action

If something looks suspicious, say that it could open the company up to legal
action. If you want a balcony repaired, tell a good sob story about that time
your friends granddad fell from a shaky balcony, and that the body corporate
had to pay out for his medical bills.

# Widows and orphans

"But what about the children!" force your opponent to look like a psychopath by
cornering their argument into looking like they're taking advantage of a
sympathetic group, ideally with a story to go along with it.
