title: Raising the floor with AI comments on policy documents
tags: [ai-safety, artificial-intelligence, governance]
---

- Proposal: when a policy document (AI-related or otherwise) opens for public
  comment, an automated AI reviewer reads the draft and looks only for win-win
  improvements.
- The reviewer pushes no cause of its own and does not shift the document's
  trade-offs. It hunts for easy wins and clear mistakes.
- Aim: catch the fixes a tired writer would have made with another day's work.
  Raise the floor and cut the number of plain errors and bad ideas that survive
  to the final draft.

Objection 1 — the win-wins don't exist:

- If true, that's a good outcome, and I'd welcome it. I don't expect it.
- Every policy document gets a limited amount of attention, so obvious win-wins
  likely slip through.
- As AI improves, it will get better at spotting these fixes: sharper wording,
  missed edge cases, and gaps that leave the document open to adversaries or
  future change.

Objection 2 — no one would accept the changes:

- The system works only if each change serves the author's own goal and makes
  the document better at reaching it.
- Most human comments try to bend a policy toward the commenter's goal, not the
  author's. This system does the opposite.
- It reads the author's stated goals and tries to make the document act on
  those goals as well as possible.
