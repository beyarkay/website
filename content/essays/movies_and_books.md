Movies are different to books. And I don't mean that in a cliché way, I mean
that the range of ideas that a book can convey is different from the range of
ideas that a movie convey. More generally, an idea is constrained by the medium
it is conveyed in.

Imagine a story at it's conception, just a thought in the creator's mind. This
story could be realised as either a film or a book[^1]. The process of
converting this story into a book or into a film is _very_ different, and has
the effect of filtering and changing stories in different ways. Compared to a
movie, a book typically has fewer people involved, fewer minds who have to
conceptualise the whole idea. Converting a story into a book will require the
book to be written down, edited, passed through the publisher, and eventually
printed. Converting a story into a movie requires a different set of processes.
At each step, the story (as it existed in the creator's mind) has a chance to
be changed or modified. Sometimes the story will be improved (such as via the
editing process), other times the story will be degraded (such as if an actor
doesn't fully understand their characters role) and other times the
modification will be somewhat neutral.

But the critical part is that there are changes, and different media have
different biases for these changes. Movies, for example, are rarely more than 3
hours long. So if you have an idea requiring more than 3 hours, it'll likely be
transformed until it's a TV show. Books typically have fewer people reading the
manuscript before it's published to the world, and so the ideas have fewer
people to convince. The "range" of a book is larger than that of a movie,
because a niche idea only has to convince the editor and publisher in order to
be made into a book, but has to convince the whole cast and crew to be made
into a movie.

Of course, this isn't entirely true. I've been describing "books" and "movies"
as homogeneous groups, but indie films are closer to books in my description
above, and mass-market Pop-Sci is closer to movies in my description above. But
there's still something here. Some media place heavy constraints on ideas that
try to go through them, due to the process of creating items in that media.
Movies are going to struggle to convey complex ideas in a faithful way, because
there are just so many more people who have to fully comprehend that idea
before a movie can be made. And comprehending ideas is difficult at scale.

One axis is just the number of people involved in the production. Books
typically have few people, but podcasts and indie films also meet this
criteria. If you need just a few people to fully realise your idea, then you're
more likely to be able to find everyone you need. Looking at all ideas which
get realised into books, it seems like books are able to express niche ideas
better than movies.

If you have a really niche idea, you'll be better able to express that idea if
you don't need to convey that idea to many people, since at the time when
you're trying to realise the idea you'll likely still be figuring it out, so
you'll be worse at explaining it than you might be to the eventual audience.

And this goes further, you and your crew might end up creating something that
does a really bad job at explaining an idea except to some very narrow
collection of people.

There's also the fact of short-form creative content like twitter tweets,
social media posts, YouTube videos, blog posts, short-form vertical content.
Even the different social media websites have different "feels" to them, and
are encouraging for different content.

If you imagine the thoughts or ideas that a movie can convey as points in
space, with similar thoughts close to one another in space, then I propose that
the volume of thought-space that books occupy is larger than the volume of
thought-space that movies occupy (although it needn't be a superset).

This proposition comes from the economics of producing one book or one movie.
Most movies require a far larger budget than most books, and also require far
more people to bring them into existence. Most books require an author, an
editor, and a publishing house, which means any budding ideas that wish to be
expressed might only need to pass though ~5 people before landing in the hands
of a reader.

Movies, on the other hand, require a massive investment and many dozens of
people. Any idea that could theoretically be expressed by a movie is hit with
filter after filter. Each person has some chance of changing the idea, and if
the idea is unusual in any way, each person has some chance of misinterpreting
that idea to be something closer to the norm.
