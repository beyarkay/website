Right now, money going towards labour is some part dumb-machine, some part
remote workers, and some part on-premise workers[numbers-needed]. Very little
work is being done by current AI systems[citation-needed], for example ChatGPT,
Claude, etc.

This raises a question: ChatGPT emerged several years ago, why are many jobs
not being automated by these systems? One answer might be that automation of
these jobs requires _some_ amount of extra effort. There needs to be someone

- Remote workers become devalued
- Property prices in large cities increase

There might be more wealth, but it almost certainly won't be shared evenly.
Almost certainly, it'll go towards the already-wealthy.

Working in software means ore things can be sold with minimal additional
labour, resulting in more wealth going towards capital than towards labour.

- AGI will first eat up jobs which are remote (but which jobs in particular?)
- AGI will first eat up jobs which are easy for AGI but relatively expensive.
  The cost of AGI will drop over time. The intelligence of AGI will rise over
  time. The number of jobs which can be automated by AGI will rise over time.

- Countries with high wages for remote workers (USA, UK, EU) will struggle
  immensely as work is either sent offshore to remote human workers in other
  countries or taken over by AGI. AGI will put a downwards pressure on
  automatable worker's wages
- Automating the legal profession will be different, because lawyers are better
  able to enact legal changes and more likely to be automated than politicians
  or legislators.
- AGI will increase demand for electricity, land/data centers near power
  plants, and political climates which are amenable to a city's worth of
  electricity going into one building.
- AI will increase the bubble effect we have seen in social media, and will
  decrease the number of novel ideas as the landscape of ideas becomes more
  homogeneous.

- AI will make it easier to infiltrate security systems, which will lead to a
  greater amount of countries / companies / communities walling themselves off
  as it becomes more and more expensive to protect themselves from foreign
  actors.

- I wouldn't be surprised if the internet fractured in the near future due to
  this increased hostility and increased danger of being connected. This
  fracturing might be global (each country or block of countries having their
  own internet, weakly connected to each other) or it might be local: more
  functionality being moved to local-first or intranet-first, with city-wide
  networks providing much of the functionality that we now expect from global
  networks such as maps, social media, etc. You might be in outrage, citing how
  the glory of the internet is in how global it is. And you'd be right, but
  you're missing that this internet I'm describing is one where the global
  internet has serious dangers, where connecting to a foreign country's network
  could lead to you losing you bank balance without being able to pursue any
  legal action. Oh wait, that already exists. But imagine if it were worse.

- High wage countries will be hit first, as their jobs get moved overseas or
  automated by AI.

- I don't think AI safety policy work is important. It doesn't seem like
  getting people interested in a cause has ever worked on a timescale less than
  multiple decades (see climate change, smoking being bad for you, nuclear
  warfare) and I think there'll be a tragedy that doesn't kill everyone but is
  enough to put AI safety on the map for the average Joe.
