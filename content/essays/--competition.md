There's an idea that's a bit tricky to explain, but quite intuitive once you've
got the shape of it, so I'm just going to start with some examples and we'll
look at what connects them afterwards.

Automotive companies compete with one another for sectors of the car market, if
a customer doesn't like what BMW is selling, they'll have a look at what
Mercedes-Benz has to offer. But these companies still cooperate to build and
maintain [IONITY][3], the European fast-charging network for electric vehicles.
It's not just BMW and Benz: Ford, Hyundai, Volkswagen, Audi, and others[^2] are
_all_ cooperating together under IONITY. What causes these companies which
compete against one another to be incentivised to work together to make IONITY?

Many submarine cables used to connect the world via the Internet are owned by
multiple competing companies. [MAREA][5] connects the US and Spain and is owned
by Microsoft and Meta. [Havfrue][6] connects the US, Ireland, and Denmark and
is owned by Meta and Google. There's also [2Africa][8] (owned by Meta,
Vodafone, and others) and [the Pacific Light Cable Network][9] (owned by Google
and Meta). Why do these companies which theoretically compete against one
another end up cooperating to fund these incredibly expensive submarine cables?

The behaviour of the corporations can somewhat be explained by Joel Spoelsky's
ideas about how companies are incentivised to [commoditise their
complement][1]: Google can both increase its profits if the internet is cheap
and fast, because if the internet is fast, more people will use Google's
products. Because of this Google is incentivised to make the internet as cheap
and as fast as possible (this also explains corporations' open-source
contributions).

However, the idea of commodotising your complement doesn't fully explain the
cooperative behaviour of competing corporations. The process of evolution has
evolved honeybees which seek out nectar for making honey and pollen as a source
of protein for larvae. In the process of collecting these products, the bees
spend some amount of energy pollinating the various flowers they interact with.
Each individual hive of bees would be better off if its bees could just spend
time collecting nectar and pollen, without wasting energy pollinating the
flowers. In the long term this would be disastrous because the population of
flowers would quickly dwindle, but in the short term the bees would be able to
produce more honey and feed more larvae. So even though the bees are not
directly incentivised to pollinate, the process of evolution has selected for
bees with this behaviour because it allows those bees to have a higher chance
of long-term survival.

Sometimes entities (like bees or corporations) can have selfish motives (like
making honey or profit) but those selfish motives are best served by pursuing
cooperative behaviour such as pollinating flowers or building submarine cables.

This is the core idea. There are some environments where agents end up pursuing
