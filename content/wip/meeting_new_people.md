- When going to a new place to meet people, long windows of opportunity are the
  _death_ of spontaneity and in general just give you a chance to chicken out.
  If you have a week in a new city and you're vaguely uncomfortable with saying
  hi to random strangers, you'll probably spend 6 days awkwardly trying to
  convince yourself to do something, and then on the last day say "fuck it" and
  actually put yourself out there. If you can, try to have concrete goals for
  each contiguous interaction, and don't let them be small achievable goals.
  You need goals that you'll fail to achieve, or else you'll choose goals too
  small to actually get anything meaningful done.

- If you're on the front lines in terms of introducing someone to a group, take
  the position seriously. How much effort you put in can be the difference
  between them doing nothing with their time in the group and them becoming a
  core member. You should ask the newbie about their interests, and make
  introductions to at least five different people, or enough that there'll
  always be someone the newbie knows at the location, and enough that they'll
  be able to find someone to click with.

- If you recognise someone from a talk they gave, make sure you have as much
  information as possible to help them connect you to that talk. The worst
  thing would be if you mention the talk, and they can't distinguish it from
  the dozens of other talks, and then what could have been an interesting
  connection is something mildly more awkward than a blind introduction.
