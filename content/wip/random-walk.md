I've had a suspicious for a while now that academic research is really a random
walk in disguise. That despite the decades of hard training acquired by
everyone in the field, researchers are really just stumbling around trying
things until something happens to work better than average.

This isn't as inflammatory as it sounds at first. _All_ search strategies
(which attempt to choose different directions to pursue or experiments to try)
lie somewhere on a continuous spectrum between uniformly randomly choosing
between all options available to the searcher, and something more biased
towards one or another of the options. With this framing, claiming that all
research is a random walk in disguise is analogous to claiming that all rocks
are sculptures, even if some of the rocks are better sculptures than others.

So you get more and less biased random walks, and some biased random walks are
biased in ways that are beneficial to the scientific endeavour. Hopefully, with
years of training and refinement, the budding academic can bias their own
random walk so as to make interesting, fruitful research more likely.
