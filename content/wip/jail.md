There's a particular argument that's common, in which someone exclaims "oh no
we couldn't possibly do that, it conflicts with our other systems!".

Why!? It's valid to say that you can't do something if an _external_ force is
preventing you from doing the thing, but completely nonsensical if there's
_internal_ pressure against doing the thing.

You see this in corporate code bases all the time: "Yeah the app is so slow
because of the way FooBarWindow.cs fetches the data, so we can't add any more
features to the window or else the problem will become even worse".

And also frequently with applications: We can't possibly give you an extension
because our internal deadline is set for this week. Impossible to change.

Despite a company about to spend hundreds of thousands of dollars on new
fellowships/employees, they're not willing to budge the deadline by 2 days to
allow for better submissions. This seems insane to me.
