AI art is problematic. This includes image generation models and video
generation models, where you can describe (in text) an image or video, and then
the AI creates that image or video for you.

These models were all trained off of enormous amounts of human-created art and
human-taken photos, the vast majority without proper licensing or permission.
This is clear because early models would happily recreate frames from the
original animated Lion King or from Avengers: Endgame. Theoretically this
shouldn't be possible because the developers haven't obtained licenses for
these works and so the models should never have seen these images. And yet they
are perfectly recreated without issue. There's also the leaked emails that show
Mark Zuckerberg explicitly allowing his AI team to use pirated textbooks to
train the Llama series of LLMs.

Being able to generate good art and visuals _is_ useful, and while it's not
unambiguously beneficial I do believe that a world with this ability is better
than a world without. But ignoring the source of the training data sets a
dangerous precedent for future AI developments.

What further complicates the issue is that some of the work was permissively
licensed in a way that legally allows AI companies to use the work for
training, but many creators felt like they did not foresee the affect of AI
training on their work, and would have not given the license had they been
aware of the full implications. Given that even AI experts have declared their
surprise at how well the transformer architecture works, and at how quickly AI
art models have become, it seems unreasonable to say "tough luck" to all the
creators who also never anticipated the advancements of a field they'd barely
heard about.

There is a solution to this problem, and it's one that might actually have a
hope of getting passed. Laws could be passed that declare all images created
before some date (say, the original release of Stable Diffusion on 22 August 2022)
are by default not permitted to be used for training AI models, even if
the original author granted a "permissive" license which would grants general
unrestricted use. Only images which explicitly permit their usage in AI
training would be granted, all other works would be denied.

This would have the effect of invalidating every image generation model created
to date, since none of them have the relevant permission from all the creators
who built the datasets. But, I believe it would not spell the end of AI image
generation. This field has shown to be immensely profitable, and so image
generation companies would be able to raise the funds required in order to
properly obtain permission to use all the artworks that went into the training
of those models.

This might look like AI companies offering royalties with individual artists,
or it might look like artists signing on with publishing companies (a bit like
the music model works today between Spotify and music companies). But I believe
that in a few years time, the AI companies would have collected a dataset of
properly licensed artworks that can be used to train the models, and the
artists who's work is being used to train the work would be properly
compensated.
